# Fan-shaped (扇形)

**In one sentence:** the side walls open outward from the stage so more people can sit with a frontal view. [S7] [S10]

## Shape
- The lateral walls open out into a spatula or fan-shaped floor, often with a curved rear wall. [S10]
- Fan-shaped rooms accommodate many listeners while keeping a frontal view of the performers. [S7]
- After the First World War, new technology and materials let architects use this shape to increase audience size. [S10]

## Advantages
- Not prone to flutter echoes, because the walls are not parallel. [S7]
- Direct sound reaches the audience easily. [S10]
- Beranek said a fan-shaped hall can work well for audiences under 800 people or, in certain circumstances, over 3,200. [S10]

## Disadvantages
- Reverberation time is generally low and clarity can be very low. [S10]
- Little lateral energy, so listeners do not feel surrounded by sound. [S10]
- Musicians find ensemble difficult, and echoes can appear from the curved rear wall. [S10]
- Note: S7 says the rear width gives good spaciousness, while S10 says the opposite; see [conflicts](../conflicts.md). [S7] [S10]

## Examples
- Barbican Hall, London, is given as a fan-shaped example by S4. [S4]
- Tanglewood Music Shed (Massachusetts) is fan-shaped yet acoustically successful, thanks to reflective upper panels. [S10]
