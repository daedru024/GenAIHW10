# The four hall types at a glance (四種廳型總覽)

**Working convention for this Wiki:** four basic shapes (shoebox, vineyard, fan-shaped, horseshoe), because one source sets out exactly these four as canonical. [S10] Other sources list different sets (see below).

| Type | Core idea | Example hall | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shoebox (鞋盒式) | Rectangular room, stage at one end | Musikverein, Vienna | [S3] [S4] [S7] [S10] |
| Vineyard (葡萄園式) | Terraced seating surrounds the stage | Berlin Philharmonie | [S4] [S7] [S8] [S10] |
| Fan-shaped (扇形) | Room widens away from the stage | Barbican, London | [S4] [S7] [S10] |
| Horseshoe (馬蹄形) | U-shaped plan with tiers of boxes | Teatro alla Scala, Milan | [S6] [S10] |

## Different sources, different lists
- S7 names shoebox, fan-shaped and vineyard as the most typical starting shapes. [S7]
- S4 contrasts vineyard with shoebox, fan-shaped and arena. [S4]
- S10 names shoebox, fan, vineyard and horseshoe, and treats "arena or vineyard" as one type. [S10]
- S6 describes La Scala's horseshoe layout but does not compare types. [S6]

## Shape is a tendency, not a guarantee
- S10 reports that a roughly rectangular hall (Philharmonic Hall) designed in 1962 sounded flat and too bright, while the fan-shaped Tanglewood hall turned out acoustically successful. [S10]
