# Clarity, spaciousness and envelopment (清晰度與包圍感)

**In one sentence:** early reflections that arrive within about 80 milliseconds make music clear, and reflections from the sides make it feel wide and surrounding. [S7]

## Clarity
- Clarity (C80) compares the sound energy arriving in the first 80 ms with the later energy. [S7]
- Later reflections blur the sound more than early ones, so clarity moves in the opposite direction to reverberation time. [S7]
- Typical C80 for concert halls is about -1 to 3 dB. [S7]
- Definition (D50) is similar but looks at the first 50 ms. [S7]
- Much of this research was done on classical music, not rhythmic music such as pop and rock. [S7]

## Spaciousness and envelopment
- Lateral energy fraction (LF) measures spaciousness: the share of early energy arriving from the sides. [S7]
- Recommended LF for concert halls is greater than 0.25. [S7]
- Wall angles and reflectors can send sound back to the audience at a very lateral angle, making the source seem wider than it is. [S7]
- Listeners feel enveloped when many early lateral reflections arrive between the direct sound and 80 ms. [S10]
- For symphonic music, lateral reflections are a priority, which is one reason rectangular halls are preferred. [S10]
- At the Musikverein, the narrow shape and thick plaster walls produce strong lateral reflections. [S3]
