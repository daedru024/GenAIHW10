# Reverberation time (殘響時間)

**In one sentence:** reverberation is how long sound keeps lingering in a room after the source stops. [S2]

## What it is
- Reverberation describes how sound energy decays in a space. [S2]
- A large gothic cathedral can take up to 9 seconds for sound to die away, while a small conference room decays very quickly. [S2]
- Longer reverberation tends to make music sound fuller but can make speech muddled. [S2]
- Musicians call a very reverberant hall "live" and a hall with little reverberation "dead". [S2]

## How it is measured
- Reverberation Time (RT) is the time for sound energy to decay by 60 decibels (dB). [S2] [S11]
- In an existing room, RT can be estimated by timing a 20 dB decay (from -5 dB to -25 dB below the peak) and multiplying by three. [S2]
- Sabine began his reverberation experiments in 1895 at Harvard's Fogg Lecture Hall and formally defined RT from them. [S11]
- RT depends on the room volume and the amount of sound absorption in it. [S2] [S11]
- To make RT longer: increase the room volume or reduce absorption. [S2]
- Designers think about air volume per person; the Berlin Philharmonie design used around 10 cubic metres per person. [S8]

## Early Decay Time (EDT)
- EDT looks at only the first 10 dB of decay, multiplied by six. [S2]
- The early part of the decay seems to predict better how an audience perceives music. [S2]
- Concert halls ideally combine a shorter EDT (for clarity) with a longer RT (for liveness). [S2]

## Typical target values (they depend on hall type and use)
| Context | RT | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Good concert halls, mid-frequencies | 1.8 to 2.2 s | [S2] |
| Symphonic music, T30 measure | 1.7 to 2.3 s | [S7] |
| Concert halls found by Sabine's studies | 2 to 2.25 s | [S11] |
| Shoebox halls (typical) | about 2 s | [S10] |
| Horseshoe opera houses | about 1.5 s | [S10] |
| Berlin Philharmonie design requirement | 2 s | [S8] |
| Weiwuying Concert Hall design | 2.1 s (mid) to 2.52 s (low frequencies) | [S9] |
| Fan-shaped halls | generally low | [S10] |
- Lecture halls were found to work best with an RT slightly under 1 second. [S11]
