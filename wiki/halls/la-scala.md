# Teatro alla Scala, Milan (horseshoe example)

**In one sentence:** a Milan opera house built to a late-18th-century Italian horseshoe pattern, valued for its acoustics. [S6]

- Built according to a recurring pattern of Italian theatres, with a horseshoe plan and several orders of boxes. [S6]
- The architect was Giuseppe Piermarini, whose design is noted for its rationality, including the sound of the room. [S6]
- Piermarini built a plaster vault on wooden ribs and left a gap between it and the real roof, working out the size of that space mathematically. [S6]
- The result is a sort of box like a musical instrument, in which sound waves circulate well. [S6]
- Today's appearance dates from the decorations of 1830. [S6]
- The stage was extended toward the audience in 1814 so performers were more visible and could use the room's acoustics. [S6]
- Type: horseshoe. [S6]
