# Berlin Philharmonie (vineyard example)

**In one sentence:** the 1963 Berlin hall that started the vineyard style, with the orchestra in the middle and terraces around it. [S4] [S8]

- Opened in 1963 and designed by Hans Scharoun; the acoustician was Lothar Cremer. [S1] [S4]
- The Great Hall has 2,440 seats. [S1] [S4]
- It replaced the old Philharmonie, which was destroyed by bombing in 1944. [S4]
- Scharoun reasoned that people naturally gather in circles to listen to music. [S4]
- Cremer was at first sceptical of placing the orchestra in the middle and worried about acoustic complications. [S8]
- The design required a reverberation time of about two seconds, which was the easiest problem because RT can be calculated. [S8]
- The harder problem was letting musicians hear each other when the platform is open on all sides. [S8]
- Fixes: reflective surfaces and balustrades on the "vineyard terraces" around the platform, and ten curved "clouds" hung 12 m above it. [S8]
- The tent-like ceiling of three convex arches helps spread sound around the hall. [S8]
- The hall's acoustics were felt to improve over time as listeners got used to it. [S8]
- Type: vineyard. [S4] [S7] [S8]
